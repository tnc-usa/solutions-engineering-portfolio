One person, thirteen production apps, and a desk full of AI agents

I run a portfolio of thirteen production applications, an internal documentation platform, and the DevOps tooling around them. Cash operations, route logistics, smart safes, ATMs, reconciliation, payments. I am one person. The way that is possible is that I build and operate primarily through AI agents, and I have built the infrastructure that makes them effective.

Let me be precise about what that does and does not mean, because the term gets abused.

What I mean by AI-native

I use AI agents (Claude Code, MCP-connected tools, subagents, and multi-agent workflows) to do the work that used to need a team around me: research, prototyping, drafting code, writing documentation, and a large share of the build itself. In practice I can stand up an integration, a demo, or a proof-of-value pilot in hours, where the old answer was weeks.

What I do not mean: I am not training models, building ML pipelines, or doing data science. I am an operator who uses applied AI as a force multiplier. The distinction matters, and I keep it honest.

The multiplier only works if you feed it well

Here is the part most people skip. An agent is only as good as the context it can see. Point one at a sprawling, undocumented estate and it guesses. So a lot of my tooling exists specifically to give agents accurate ground truth.

The self-documenting pipeline I built keeps every app's schema current and chunked, so an agent pulls precise context instead of drowning in it. The live-versus-cloud indexer keeps the code an agent reasons about in sync with what is actually deployed. The point of that infrastructure is not tidiness for its own sake. It is that it turns a general-purpose agent into one that knows my systems.

Why this is the right shape for a small operation

A solo operator running production software for real businesses cannot afford a team's worth of overhead, and cannot afford to be slow. AI agents close that gap, but only if the work is disciplined: versioned, documented, idempotent, safe to re-run. The same rigor that makes a system maintainable is what makes it legible to an agent. They reinforce each other.

What I take from it

I am not selling AI. I am showing what a builder with the right tooling can carry alone: a production portfolio that would normally need a small team, kept to a standard, shipped fast. If you want someone who can walk into your stack, get to useful output in hours, and bring the engineering discipline that keeps AI-assisted work honest, that is what I do. The agents are the multiplier. The judgment about what to build, and the discipline to build it properly, is still the job.
